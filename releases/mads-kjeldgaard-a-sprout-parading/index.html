Press release

In short: A Sprout Parading is an upbeat computer music album by Danish composer Mads Kjeldgaard. It is released digitally and on CD via Kjeldgaard’s own Exformal Records on December 1st, 2026 (with CD’s shipping in end of 2026/start of 2027).

A Sprout Parading was recorded in a time of grief, loss and love, and is defiantly uplifting about it all. It marks a turning point in the musical output of Kjeldgaard, who has spent years searching for a new form of expression in electronic music. The result is a playful, adventurous, and sometimes silly exploration of music at the structural and sonic levels — music born of an experimental process that has clearly been a lot of fun for the composer.

Throughout the record, the music is melodically inspired by the canons in Bach’s ‘Goldberg Variations’ (itself the result of a seemingly playful process) as well as Steve Reich’s developmental melodies (as heard in early pieces like ‘Drumming’ or ‘Music for a Large Ensemble’). The melodic brightness of the pieces are due to the extensive use of pentatonic major scales, often criticised in Western music theory books for ‘lacking direction’ (a problem that becomes a feature in this music where the point exactly is not to supply a clear harmonic storyline but rather to create tonally vibrant states of being), and at the same time massively influential and widespread in many kinds of folk music all around the world; gamelan music, also a big influence on the music here, is a classic example of this.

The songs on A Sprout Parading were composed using Mads Kjeldgaard’s Modular Algorithmic Sound System (MASS), a composition and performance system for electronic music that allows the user to interact with music on the structural level. On a typical electronic music instrument, the user tweaks a parameter that changes the sound directly, like the filter cutoff of a synthesizer that regulates the amount of overtones in a signal.

In MASS, parameters instead represent structural features of the music itself on a compositional level — one parameter, for example, allows choosing between different styles of harmonic cadences, another harmonizes a melody, a third may rotate voices in a cannon. The goal is to turn large scale compositional choices into interactions that are as accessible as tweaking a filter cutoff. The pieces heard on this record are all recordings of an experimental process where Kjeldgaard improvises with this system and thus with the composition process itself.

The release also features analog photography by Mads Kjeldgaard.

We hope you will enjoy :)

About Mads Kjeldgaard

Mads Kjeldgaard is a composer from Denmark. He is the founder of the record label Exformal Records and is a member of The Danish Composers’ Society.
